# Design Theory Framework

Use this audit on every Design Family and every SKU. A sentence such as “looks premium” is not evidence.

| Dimension | Required decision | Failure signal |
|---|---|---|
| Semantic Coherence | Name one theme/emotion and show how motif, type, color, material, composition, and process serve it. | Elements tell different stories. |
| Style Coherence | Identify the shared historical or visual language and any intentional contrast. | Style soup or decorative novelty without a rule. |
| Visual Hierarchy | Name primary, secondary, accent, first focus, second focus, and supporting information. | Name, date, motif, and border compete. |
| Gestalt | State how proximity, similarity, continuity, closure, figure-ground, or common region unifies the design. | Objects appear parked independently. |
| Balance | Identify symmetry/asymmetry/radial logic, visual weight, center of gravity, edge tension, and flow. | One edge feels accidental or unstable. |
| Negative Space | Reserve blank zones, text safety, motif-to-edge breathing room, and removals. | Surface is filled to signal value. |
| Proportion, Scale, Rhythm | Define motif/product ratio, name/date ratio, repetition, spacing, density, and long-name response. | Same scale is used for every input. |
| Typography | Assign roles, readability, size/length behavior, case, spacing, and process fit. | Decorative type hides information. |
| Color | Define hue/value/saturation/temperature/contrast/harmony and material/process behavior. | Low contrast or render-only colors. |
| Material Honesty | Show that the material and process can produce the visual promise. | Mockup implies color, depth, gloss, or detail not available. |
| Function and Ergonomics | Protect grip, cutting, cleaning, zipper, hinge, opening, button, load, safety, and lifetime as applicable. | Decoration interferes with use. |
| Personalization Usability | Define length, characters, proof policy, error risks, and invalid combinations. | Buyer can order content the layout cannot hold. |
| Commercial Feasibility | Connect buyer, occasion, price band, visual explanation, batch size, labor, margin direction, and returns. | Concept is attractive but hard to explain or produce. |

## Audit method

For each dimension record: decision, evidence, risk, adjustment, and score 1–5. Any critical dimension below 3 invokes redesign. A Design Family passes only when its rules still work across short/long personalization and the allowed process.

## Design Family coherence test

Remove the Product name and look only at motif language, type roles, palette, composition, and process. The SKUs should still look related. Then compare families: each must differ in buyer promise or visual system, not merely in motif species or color.

## SKU distinctness test

A SKU must change a meaningful concept structure: buyer/occasion, semantic focus, hierarchy, motif role and treatment, composition, placement strategy, personalization structure, material/process identity, or market position. One isolated swap is an option, not a new Base SKU.

Before release, compare every pair of sibling SKUs within a Design Family using [the SKU distinctness matrix](../templates/sku-distinctness-matrix.md). Each pair must differ on at least two meaningful axes, including at least one of semantic focus, hierarchy, composition, personalization structure, production identity, or market position. Motif species, font, or color swaps alone fail and must be collapsed to P/F/C options.
