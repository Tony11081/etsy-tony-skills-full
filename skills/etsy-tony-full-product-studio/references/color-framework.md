# Color Framework

Analyze hue, value, saturation, temperature, contrast, harmony, material reflection, background relationship, text readability, and process limitations.

## Layers

Keep distinct:

- Product Base Color
- Main Graphic Color
- Secondary Graphic Color
- Text Color
- Thread Color
- Engraving Appearance
- Border Color
- Finish Color

An engraving appearance caused by substrate/marking is not automatically a selectable ink color. A finish change that affects procurement, setup, cost, durability, or photography is normally a Separate SKU.

## Selection method

1. Start from confirmed material/base color and viewing distance.
2. Set a minimum practical contrast target through sample review; do not invent numeric ratios for non-screen materials.
3. Choose a family palette that supports its semantic promise.
4. Remove colors the process cannot reproduce consistently.
5. Assign C-codes only to actually sourceable/testable choices.
6. Require a swatch or finished-sample disclaimer: screens and physical products may differ.

## Process prompts

- Engraving/marking: contrast depends on material, coating, oxidation, depth, and light; sample it.
- Embroidery: use stocked thread colors, background contrast, sheen, and stitch density; record lot changes.
- DTF/DTG/screen/UV: consider white underbase, gamut, opacity, trapping, adhesion, and cure.
- Sublimation: substrate/coating and base color control appearance.
- Transparent acrylic: white ink and front/reverse order materially change color.

Avoid opening color choices that require new material procurement or separate process setup unless their commercial value supports a Separate SKU or Add-on.
