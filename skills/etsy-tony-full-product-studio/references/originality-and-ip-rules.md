# Originality and IP Rules

Learn the design grammar, not the finished design.

## May learn

Product category, function, usage scene, buyer need, generic subject matter, broad style, composition principles, market preferences, and category-standard structure.

## Must not copy

Complete illustration, distinctive composition, distinctive motif combination, original copy, special font pairing, brand name, logo, character, trademark, film/anime/game element, lyrics, recognizable artwork, competitor proprietary graphic, distinctive personalization structure, or overall visual impression.

## Originality Distance Check

Compare:

1. overall impression
2. main motif
3. motif treatment
4. composition
5. typography pairing
6. color system
7. placement
8. personalization format
9. decorative structure
10. copywriting
11. photography direction

Classify similarity as Category, Functional, Generic Style, or Distinctive Expression. Distinctive Expression similarity fails. Category or functional similarity is acceptable only when the new work does not preserve the competitor’s particular expression.

Materially change several applicable axes: silhouette/geometry, component layout, function execution, motif/surface language, material/finish, color, proportion, assembly, personalization system, and included-piece relationship. Four changed axes are a useful screen, not automatic clearance; overall impression still controls.

## Search and reporting

When a suspicious name, phrase, logo, character, or branded feature appears and search is available, check current official trademark/copyright databases and authoritative sources. Record query, jurisdiction, class/category relevance, date, result, and uncertainty. Search absence is not clearance.

When search is unavailable, state IP Verification Required. Never claim zero or absolute infringement risk.

## Remediation

If distance fails, do not “fix” it with color/font/flower swaps. Rebuild the family narrative, hierarchy, composition, motif treatment, personalization structure, and photography direction from the buyer job and process constraints.
