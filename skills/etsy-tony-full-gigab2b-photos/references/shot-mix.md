# 15-Shot Mix

Use this shot mix after extracting the GigaB2B product title and reference images. Adjust scenes to the actual product category, and use 15 as the default count. A user-requested count overrides this example; adapt the mix without duplicating roles. Human/pet suggestions below are optional and subject to the photography engine's scale and safety checks.

1. Clean ecommerce hero on a neutral bright background.
2. Premium lifestyle scene with one person naturally using or reaching for the product.
3. Lifestyle scene with a pet or animal only when it fits the product and would not feel random; otherwise use a category-appropriate non-human scale cue.
4. Alternate three-quarter angle showing shape and depth.
5. Side or profile view that clarifies thickness, silhouette, or construction.
6. Detail close-up in a real lifestyle scene, focusing on the most important material, surface, connector, stitch, hardware, label, or finish.
7. In-use scene in the most natural room, outdoor area, office, studio, patio, kitchen, bedroom, workshop, or vehicle context for the product.
8. Scale-context shot with a person, hand, furniture, pet, or familiar object that fits the category.
9. Giftable or styled listing photo with tasteful category-relevant props.
10. Secondary lifestyle scene with different camera distance and product orientation.
11. Practical-use photo that shows the buyer why the product solves a need.
12. Clean catalog angle with minimal props for marketplace listing use.
13. Construction or rear/underside/open/assembled view when the reference images support it.
14. Warm lived-in scene with tasteful signs of real use, but no mess or random clutter.
15. Product dimension diagram based on the GigaB2B dimension reference image.

Dimension-shot rules:

- Use the GigaB2B dimension image as the only source for measurements.
- Preserve exact numbers, units, arrows, and relative product proportions when readable.
- If measurements are not readable, ask for a clearer source image instead of inventing values.
- A dimension image may include clean text labels, arrows, and measurement lines because the user explicitly requested it.

Lifestyle rules:

- People should look natural and secondary to the product.
- Animals should appear only when plausible for the product category or buyer context.
- Keep lighting neutral and product colors accurate.
- Avoid props or scenes that make the product look cheap, unsafe, messy, or unrelated.
- Detail shots must not use a plain white background. Put the detail in a believable scene, such as beside a bed, on a tabletop, in a hand, near daily-use props, or in the product's natural use environment.
