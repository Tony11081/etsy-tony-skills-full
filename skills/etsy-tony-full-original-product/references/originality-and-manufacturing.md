# Originality and Manufacturing Review

Read this reference when decomposing the Etsy source, creating concepts, and auditing similarity.

## Inspiration Boundary

| Treatment | Examples |
|---|---|
| May inspire | buyer problem, intended function, generic construction principle, common material class, category-standard interface, broad lighting principle, broad camera role |
| Must be redesigned | overall silhouette, component layout, proportions, ornamental system, surface treatment, distinctive color combination, customization system, assembly expression, scene composition |
| Never copy | brand name, logo, watermark, seller photo, listing copy, artwork, graphic pattern, character, exact text, engraving, signature motif, unique ornament placement, model identity |

Functional similarity is allowed only when the new product has its own visual and construction identity. The safest concept keeps the buyer job while changing how the product expresses and delivers that function.

## Distinctive-Detail Test

Put a detail in `must_not_copy` when any answer is yes:

- Would a buyer recognize the original seller or design from this detail alone?
- Is it artistic, ornamental, branded, textual, character-based, or unusually arranged?
- Does the detail dominate the product's overall impression?
- Is it unnecessary for the basic function?
- Would moving, recoloring, or resizing it still leave the design recognizably the same?

Small does not mean generic. Hardware geometry, stitching paths, engraving placement, border motifs, and relief can be distinctive.

## Originality Axes

Evaluate these axes and record the concrete change:

1. overall silhouette or geometry
2. component count and layout
3. functional interaction or mechanism
4. motif, pattern, or surface language
5. material and finish system
6. color relationship
7. proportions and dimensions
8. assembly or joinery expression
9. personalization method
10. included-piece or bundle relationship

Changing four axes is a minimum gate, not automatic proof of originality. Reject a concept when the overall impression remains substantially similar.

## Manufacturing Evidence Table

Use this structure for every process claim:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Fact or proposal | Specific material, process, join, finish, tolerance, or supplier capability |
| Evidence | visible source fact, seller claim, user fact, supplier fact, category inference, or unknown |
| Confidence | high, medium, or low |
| Production implication | equipment, tooling, labor, lead time, MOQ, quality control, or packaging consequence |
| Validation action | prototype test, supplier quotation, sample inspection, material certificate, fit test, drop test, wash test, or other concrete check |

Do not describe an item as easy to make solely because it looks simple. Consider pattern or mold development, tolerances, finishing, assembly time, fragile parts, packaging, regulatory labeling, and defect risk.

## Concept Sheet Contract

Each concept sheet must include:

- `concept_id`
- `working_name`
- `buyer_job`
- `original_design_idea`
- `source_function_retained`
- `design_axes_changed`
- `source_elements_excluded`
- `materials_and_parts`
- `process_sequence`
- `equipment_or_supplier_type`
- `prototype_tests`
- `manufacturability_rating`
- `shipping_and_packaging`
- `similarity_risk`
- `unknowns`

## Scene Inspiration Boundary

Abstract parameters that may inspire a new photograph:

- soft versus hard light
- front, side, or back lighting
- wide, medium, close, macro, top-down, eye-level, or low-angle role
- shallow versus deep focus
- clean studio, lived-in home, workshop, gifting, installation, or use narrative
- warm, cool, neutral, high-key, or low-key mood
- use of negative space, scale cue, hand interaction, or material detail proof

Do not reproduce the source's combined location, prop cluster, product placement, model styling, pose, crop, camera angle, lighting direction, and palette. Change at least three applicable elements and require a clearly different overall composition.

## Similarity Audit

Compare each concept and final scene against every source image:

- function: may be similar
- generic category cues: may be similar
- silhouette and proportions: should be materially different unless function requires a standard geometry
- component arrangement: should be materially different
- artwork, pattern, motif, engraving, logo, and text: no copying
- materials and colors: use only if approved for the new product; avoid distinctive combinations
- overall product impression: must be different
- scene composition: must be different
- model identity, outfit, pose, and prop cluster: must be different

Mark each item `pass`, `borderline`, or `reject`. Only `pass` proceeds. `borderline` requires revision rather than user acceptance when the risk concerns copying or unsupported product facts.
