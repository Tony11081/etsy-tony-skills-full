# Customization Architecture

Evaluate twelve groups: Pattern, Font, Color, Personalization Content, Placement, Size, Material, Finish, Production Process, Add-on, Packaging, and Gift Message.

Every group is one of:

- Fixed
- Customer Selectable
- Conditional
- Add-on
- Separate SKU
- Separate Listing
- Not Offered

## Option admission test

Open an option only when:

1. buyers understand it without professional vocabulary;
2. images can explain it;
3. production can identify it with a code;
4. compatibility can be enforced;
5. it preserves aesthetics and function;
6. it has commercial value;
7. it does not create disproportionate proof, error, lead-time, or return risk.

If any answer fails, fix it, make it conditional, charge as an Add-on, split it, or do not offer it.

## Personalization fields

For First Name, Last Name, Couple Names, Initials, Monogram, Dates, Family Name, City, State, Coordinates, Short Quote, Message, Year, Pet Name, or Child Name, record required/optional, recommended and maximum tested length, over-limit handling, date format, case, punctuation, supported characters, spelling confirmation, preview, and proof policy.

Never open content the physical artwork area cannot hold.

## SKU boundary

- Names/dates normally stay personalization inputs.
- Pattern alternatives sharing one construction/layout may be P-codes.
- A new material, finish, process, size file, tooling path, or QA plan may require a Separate SKU.
- A different Design Family, buyer intent, scene, search intent, price band, or ordering logic normally requires a Separate Listing.

## Option-count control

Count visible choices and invalid pairs. Prefer two or three tested fonts, a small compatible pattern set, one fixed placement, one fixed process, and only sourceable colors/finishes. More options require evidence of value and a stronger dependency/preview system.
