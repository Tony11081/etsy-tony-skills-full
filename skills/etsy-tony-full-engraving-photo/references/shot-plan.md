# Fixed 15-Image Etsy Shot Plan

Use this plan after the product master passes its quality gate. Generate every item with one separate Codex built-in `image_gen` call. Never use CLI batch generation, a third-party generator, or a contact sheet. Keep one product identity and one personalization across the full set.

## Suite rules

- Use at least three camera distances: macro, medium and wide.
- Use overhead, three-quarter, eye-level and low-detail angles across the set.
- Do not reuse the same background and prop arrangement for more than two shots.
- Keep props secondary and never cover the engraved zones in engraving-focused shots.
- Prefer hands or partial body for interaction scenes. Avoid a visible face unless requested.
- Keep the product naturally grounded with plausible contact shadows and reflective metal.
- Use the approved product master for every shot; never chain one generated scene into the next.

## Shots

### 01 — Primary wedding hero

- Show both utensils prominently beside an elegant wedding cake.
- Use a refined reception-table setting, soft natural or editorial light and a three-quarter angle.
- Keep the full set readable at thumbnail size and leave breathing room around it.

### 02 — Clean studio hero

- Show the complete set against a warm ivory, pale stone or other quiet neutral surface.
- Use a clean overhead or shallow three-quarter view with minimal props.
- Make this a product-first alternative cover.

### 03 — Editorial flat lay

- Arrange both utensils with restrained wedding details such as an invitation edge, ribbon, rings or a small floral sprig.
- Use a true overhead camera and a different composition from Shot 02.
- Do not let props obscure handles, serrations or engraving.

### 04 — Knife engraving close-up

- Fill the frame with the knife blade and its engraving while retaining enough handle/serration context to prove product identity.
- Use grazing light that shows subtle laser etching and real steel reflections.
- Include the original design source as an additional reference.

### 05 — Server engraving close-up

- Fill the frame with the wide cake server and its main personalization.
- Keep names, skyline, date and line art exact and fully legible.
- Include the original design source as an additional reference.

### 06 — Handle and material macro

- Focus on the ornate embossed floral handles, collars, polished metal and craftsmanship details.
- Keep part of both utensils visible when practical.
- Do not invent a different handle pattern.

### 07 — Cutting-the-cake interaction

- Show natural hands using the engraved knife to cut a wedding cake.
- Preserve realistic grip, utensil scale and blade direction.
- Keep faces out of frame by default and do not cover all personalization.

### 08 — Serving-the-slice interaction

- Show natural hands lifting or serving a slice with the engraved cake server.
- Use a different angle, background and moment from Shot 07.
- Keep the wide server physically plausible beneath the slice.

### 09 — Cake-side product moment

- Rest the full set or one foreground utensil naturally beside a plated cake slice.
- Use a medium close perspective with appetizing but restrained styling.
- Keep food away from the engraved zones.

### 10 — Reception lifestyle wide

- Place the set in a wider wedding reception or sweetheart-table scene.
- Make the product clearly identifiable while showing the emotional use context.
- Use a setting not already used in Shots 01–09.

### 11 — Gift presentation

- Present the two-piece set as a premium personalized gift in or beside a tasteful unbranded box.
- Use clean tissue, ribbon or a card without adding readable invented copy.
- Do not hide blade shapes or alter the product to fit packaging.

### 12 — Personalization proof

- Show a crisp, product-first view that makes both engraving zones easy to inspect together.
- Use a neutral background and generous empty space suitable for later listing annotations.
- Add no new text overlays, labels, arrows or badges during image generation.

### 13 — Size or scale proof

- If verified dimensions exist, create a clean dimension view using only those exact values and simple non-overlapping lines.
- Otherwise show the set beside a natural hand or standard cake plate for relative scale, with no numbers.
- Never estimate length, width or package dimensions.

### 14 — Venue or occasion story

- Match the engraving story when possible: city/venue design in an elegant urban setting, botanical design in a garden setting, birthday design at a celebration table.
- Use a wider environmental composition unlike Shot 10.
- Keep the product and personalization unchanged; only the scene may adapt.

### 15 — Alternate thumbnail cover

- Create a bold, centered, uncluttered final cover option with the complete set large in frame.
- Use a different surface, light direction and camera angle from Shots 01 and 02.
- Prioritize instant product recognition and readable personalization.

## Failure handling

- If engraving changes, regenerate the failed shot from the product master plus original engraving design.
- If the product changes, restate its fingerprint before the shot brief: exactly two utensils, top serrated knife, wide server, ornate embossed floral handles and reflective stainless steel.
- If a hand is malformed, crop to a simpler hand interaction or show hands farther from the camera.
- If a shot becomes a collage, explicitly request one photograph, one camera view and one moment.
- If dimension lines overlap or values drift, remove the dimension graphic and use the no-number scale fallback.
