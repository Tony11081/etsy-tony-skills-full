# Design Theory Framework

Apply P1 and P3 from `../SKILL.md`. Use this framework for every STANDARD/FULL Design Family and SKU. “Looks premium” or “looks balanced” is not a design decision unless the response identifies the visual mechanism and evidence.

## Required Design Family questions

Every Family must answer:

1. What emotion and story does the system express?
2. Why are these motifs appropriate to the buyer and occasion?
3. Why do the typography roles belong to the same system?
4. Why do the color/value/finish choices work on the real material?
5. Why does the composition fit the physical Product?
6. What is the first visual focus?
7. What is the second visual focus?
8. Which zones must remain empty?
9. Why is the material appropriate?
10. Why is the process appropriate?
11. Why do all elements share one visual language?
12. Does the design still work after short, long, or multi-part personalization?

## Theory dimensions

| Dimension | Required decision | Typical failure | Useful correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Semantic Coherence | Define one buyer promise; connect Product, motif, type, material, process, and occasion to it. | Decoration tells a different story from the purchase reason. | Remove unrelated symbols; rebuild from buyer job. |
| Style Coherence | Define a shared period, craft, geometry, or visual language and any intentional contrast. | Style soup; vintage motif with unrelated novelty font. | Establish one anchor and one controlled secondary language. |
| Visual Hierarchy | Name primary, secondary, and supporting information plus first and second focus. | Names, date, motif, and border compete equally. | Change scale, contrast, position, density, or type role. |
| Gestalt | State how proximity, similarity, continuity, closure, figure-ground, or common region unifies the layout. | Objects appear parked independently. | Group related content and create a continuous reading path. |
| Composition | Define axis, balance, visual weight, edge tension, and flow on the actual surface. | Artwork fits the mockup but not the usable zone. | Recompose to the measured zone and protect function. |
| Negative Space | Reserve breathing room, text safety, edge clearance, and protected functional space. | Surface is filled to signal value. | Remove secondary detail before shrinking important content. |
| Proportion | Define Product-to-art, motif-to-type, and primary-to-secondary ratios. | One layout is stretched onto every size or name length. | Create controlled short/medium/long states. |
| Rhythm | Define repetition, intervals, density changes, and directional cadence. | Decorative repeats become noise or accidental gaps. | Use a repeat rule or reduce repetition. |
| Typography | Assign roles, legibility, case, spacing, length response, glyph needs, and process fit. | Decorative type hides the buyer's information. | Use a readable fallback, fewer roles, and tested minimums. |
| Color | Define hue, value, saturation, temperature, contrast, harmony, and real process/material behavior. | Mockup implies colors or contrast the process cannot make. | Use verified finish/process behavior; otherwise mark Sample Required. |

## Family coherence and distinctness

Remove the Product name and compare motif treatment, typography roles, color/value system, composition, negative-space rules, and process language. SKUs inside a Family should still look related. Different Families must differ in customer promise or overall visual system, not just flower species, font, or color.

A distinct SKU changes a meaningful concept structure, such as semantic focus, hierarchy, composition, personalization structure, material/process identity, or market position. An isolated motif, font, or color swap is an option or redesign, not a new SKU.

## Design Gate scoring

Score exactly these seven dimensions from 1–5:

- Semantic Coherence
- Style Coherence
- Visual Hierarchy
- Composition
- Negative Space
- Typography
- Color

Use the following anchors:

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 5 | Intentional, evidence-linked, resilient across allowed inputs, and ready for sampling within stated process limits. |
| 4 | Coherent and usable; only routine refinement or sampling remains. |
| 3 | Conditional; one visible weakness needs a defined redesign or test. |
| 2 | Major conflict or instability. |
| 1 | No defensible design system or a direct contradiction. |

Decision rules:

- Any dimension below 3: Design Gate fails.
- Average below 3.5: not eligible for recommendation.
- Average at least 3.5 with a visible weakness: `Redesign Recommended`.
- Top 3 candidates should normally average at least 4.0.

Record all seven component scores and the arithmetic average to two decimals. Gestalt, proportion, and rhythm remain mandatory narrative checks even though they are not separate ranking fields in Phase 1.

## Failed Design redesign rules

For every failed candidate output:

- Failed Dimension
- Evidence or observed symptom
- Why It Failed
- Required Redesign
- Downstream process/customization impact
- Re-test condition

Correct the cause, not just the appearance:

- Semantic failure: rebuild from buyer, occasion, and emotional promise.
- Style failure: remove incompatible languages; define one anchor.
- Hierarchy failure: assign primary/secondary roles and reduce competition.
- Composition/space failure: remeasure the artwork zone and create length/size states.
- Typography failure: change role, structure, spacing, or tested fallback; do not merely pick a prettier font.
- Color failure: reconnect contrast to actual material and process; remove unverified mockup effects.
